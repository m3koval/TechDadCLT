Independence comes first

Support should feel
like support.

You choose what changes, who receives information and whether a family member is involved. A relative can help arrange or pay for a visit; your permission still matters.

  • Accounts you can recover

    Review recovery options and appropriate passkeys or two-step verification. You enter credentials yourself.

  • Calls and messages with less pressure

    Review supported spam controls without losing important calls. Practice a simple known-number callback routine.

  • A trusted-contact plan

    Choose who can help and what they may see. Use explicit vendor invitations where available, not shared passwords.

  • Instructions that stay with you

    For agreed setup work, a readable handoff with changes, remaining limitations and next steps you can practice.

A habit worth keeping

Pause.
Check.
Call back.

An urgent message is a reason to slow down—not hand over access.

End the call or message. Contact the person or organization through a number you already trust, such as the number on your bank card. Caller ID and a familiar-sounding voice are not proof.
