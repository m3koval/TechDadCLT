Five parental-control checks worth making

You don’t need to change every setting tonight. Start with the account, then check what your child can install, access and share.

1. Check the account and family group

Use a child account with the correct age and supported family supervision. On Apple, review Family Sharing and Screen Time; enable Share Across Devices where appropriate and verify settings on each device. Protect Screen Time with a parent-only passcode.

Google Family Link supervision does not automatically end at 13. The applicable account age varies by country; Google’s current guidance says under-18s need parent approval to stop supervision. Follow the current account-transition instructions rather than removing controls based on a birthday alone.

2. Make time limits intentional

Choose downtime and app limits around your routine. Decide what stays available for school and contacting trusted people. Test the result on the child’s device. Microsoft Family Safety supports device limits on Windows/Xbox and supported app/game limits; the parent iOS app does not provide general child iOS enforcement.

3. Review web and video settings separately

Supervised Google SafeSearch is normally on and locked to Filter by default; verify it rather than assuming it was off. Signed-out sessions and other apps can have different coverage. Choose YouTube Kids or a supported supervised YouTube experience appropriate to age. YouTube Restricted Mode is not the same as a supervised experience.

Apple’s menu names vary by OS. Use the official parental-controls instructions for that version to review web content, app installation and purchase restrictions. Do not treat a time limit as a guaranteed app ban.

4. Look inside games—not just at the router

In Roblox, link a parent account or use supported Parent mode. Review content maturity, communication, spending and time settings. Restrict or disable supported chat as appropriate; do not weaken filtering as a safety step.

Communication eligibility, trusted-friend features and age checks vary. Some controls exclude creator-added chat, so test the actual experience. On consoles, use the manufacturer’s supported family controls; network blocking does not manage offline play or every in-game conversation.

5. Review social communication together

Instagram Teen Accounts include default safeguards. Linked supervision can show recent message contacts, not all message text; younger teens need permission for some less-protective settings. TikTok DMs are available only to registered users aged 16 or older; Family Pairing can restrict eligible teens’ settings.

Apple Communication Safety offers on-device protection in supported surfaces. It is not a dashboard of all third-party chats. Explain to children what you can see, what you cannot, and how they can ask for help without losing every privilege.

Finish with a real check. Try the important rules on home Wi-Fi and cellular, after a restart, and with every relevant account. Keep notes on what worked and what remains unsupported.
